Trip Planning
Three Itinerary Options

All options share these constants: late Day 1 start (Marblemount permit pickup + Seattle drive), island camps as primary targets on the lake with named alternates if unavailable on April 15, and at least one night at Desolation Camp on the mountain. Each day shows a Primary camp and an Alternate.

April 15 booking priority: Cat Island (4 sites) and Ten Mile Island (3 sites) are the island targets — book these first.
Option A · 6 Days
Classic Island Route
Ten Mile Island northbound, Cat Island as Desolation base, one night on the mountain. Clean and unhurried.
6 nights ~28 mi paddling 1 summit night Island camps priority
1
Day
Marblemount → Midlake (4–6 mi)
Pick up permits at Marblemount WIC (opens 8am). Drive to SR-20 milepost 134, hike 1 mi down to the dock, call resort for shuttle. Launch by midday. Short first paddle — get comfortable with the kayaks, stay ahead of afternoon winds. Explore Big Beaver Creek canyon by kayak once camp is set.
🚣 4–6 mi paddle
PrimaryBig Beaver — 4.6 mi, 7 sites, dock. Largest camp on south half; easy creek exploration.
AlternateCougar Island — 2.4 mi, 2 sites. Shorter if very late start; island means no bears.
2
Day
Push North to Ten Mile Island (~5–8 mi)
Full paddling day — early start. Pass Rainbow Point and Devil's Junction. Ten Mile Island sits mid-lake with open-water views in all directions and natural wind buffer. Good position for the Desolation push north tomorrow.
🚣 5–8 mi paddle
PrimaryTen Mile Island — 10 mi from resort, 3 sites, no dock. Island camp, open lake views. ~3 mi from Desolation dock.
AlternateDry Creek — 10.4 mi, 4 sites, dock. Just past Ten Mile; solid fallback if island is booked.
AlternatePonderosa — 11 mi, 2 sites, dock. Another fallback; pleasant camp, ~2 mi from Desolation dock.
3
Day
Ten Mile → Lightning Creek, then Desolation Camp
Short paddle north to the Desolation dock (~2–4 mi from Ten Mile Island). Stash kayaks and begin ascent early. Hike all the way to Desolation Camp (~4.2 mi, just below summit) — no hurry to come back down today. Visit Jim at the lookout in the late afternoon. First night on the mountain.
🚣 2–4 mi to dock
🥾 ~4.2 mi to Desolation Camp
SummitDesolation Camp — 1 mi below summit. Separate NPS permit required (recreation.gov). No camping at lookout itself.
Carry 4–6L water per person from the lake. No reliable sources on the trail above. Start the hike no later than 8am.
4
Day
Summit Morning → Descend → Cat Island
Sunrise from Desolation Camp. One more visit to Jim and the lookout in the morning light. Descend to the Desolation dock (~5.1 mi), retrieve kayaks, and paddle 1 mile south to Cat Island. Rest and recover on the island — swim, watch the lake, do absolutely nothing.
🥾 5.1 mi descent
🚣 ~1 mi south
PrimaryCat Island — 13 mi, 4 sites, dock. Classic island camp at foot of Desolation ridge. Iconic post-summit recovery.
AlternateLightning Creek Boat Camp — stay put at the dock camp if legs are done. Best facilities on the lake.
5
Day
Cat Island → Rainbow Point (7 mi south)
Rest day behind you, full paddle day south. Pass mid-lake coves, waterfalls tumbling off the western slopes, and the Devil's Creek canyon. Rainbow Point has East Bank Trail access for an evening walk if the legs are willing.
🚣 ~7 mi paddle
PrimaryRainbow Point — 6 mi from resort, 3 sites, dock. Good staging for the final paddle home.
AlternateBig Beaver — 4.6 mi, 7 sites, dock. Stop earlier if tired; more campsite options.
6
Day
Home — Rainbow Point → Resort (6 mi)
Final morning paddle back to the resort dock. Return kayaks, catch the shuttle across to the lower dock, hike 1 mi up to the trailhead parking. Drive home on SR-20 with the North Cascades in the rearview.
🚣 6 mi paddle
🎯 Return to resort
Option B · 7 Days
Two Nights on the Mountain
The Jim Version — two nights at Desolation Camp, sunrise and sunset from the lookout, unhurried time with an old friend. West shore exploration on the return.
7 days ~28 mi paddling 2 summit nights West shore exploration
1
Day
Marblemount → Big Beaver (4–5 mi)
7 days means no need to rush, but push a little further than Option A's Day 1 — positioning for a longer northbound push on Day 2. Get on the water, get comfortable, arrive well before winds build. Big Beaver Creek kayak exploration in the evening.
🚣 4–5 mi paddle
PrimaryBig Beaver — 4.6 mi, 7 sites, dock. Most options south of mid-lake; creek to explore.
AlternateCougar Island — 2.4 mi, 2 sites. If very late start; island setting, no bears.
2
Day
Push North — Ten Mile Island or Cat Island (8–13 mi)
The big paddling day. Early start mandatory. Two strong targets: Cat Island (13 mi) puts you 1 mile from the Desolation dock — a short Day 3 morning before the ascent. Ten Mile Island (10 mi) is the safer distance with a ~4 mi paddle to the dock on Day 3. Either works; Cat Island is the bolder choice if conditions are good.
🚣 8–13 mi paddle
PrimaryCat Island — 13 mi, 4 sites, dock. 1 mile from Desolation dock. Positions you perfectly for an easy Day 3 morning paddle before the ascent.
AlternateTen Mile Island — 10 mi, 3 sites, no dock. Island views, ~4 mi from dock. Solid if Cat Island is unavailable or conditions shorten the day.
3
Day
Ten Mile → Lightning Creek → Desolation Camp (Night 1)
Short paddle to the Desolation dock (~2–4 mi from Ten Mile Island), then begin the ascent. Take it at a comfortable pace — you have two nights up there. Settle into Desolation Camp and head up to the lookout in the late afternoon. First sunset with Jim.
🚣 2–4 mi to dock
🥾 ~4.2 mi to camp
Summit Night 1Desolation Camp — Separate NPS permit required. Carry 4–6L water from the lake. No sources on trail.
April 15 booking priority: secure 2 consecutive Desolation Camp nights first — those dates drive the entire itinerary. Everything else gets built around them.
4
Day
Full Day on the Mountain (Summit Night 2)
Sunrise from Desolation Camp. This is the day you came for — no schedule, no paddle ahead of you. Morning at the lookout. Afternoon wandering the summit ridge. Long conversation with Jim. Watch the weather systems move through the North Cascades from 6,085 feet. Read Kerouac. Sunset again. Two nights makes the difference between passing through and actually being there.
🥾 Short ridge walks
Summit Night 2Desolation Camp — Second permit night. Resupply water by descending ~1.5 mi to the trickle stream (unreliable in July — carry extra).
5
Day
Descend → Cat Island
Morning descent — full 9.3 mi back to the Desolation dock, 4,543 ft down. Retrieve kayaks and paddle 1 mile south to Cat Island. Pure recovery mode: swim off the dock, lie in the sun, eat everything. The island camp after a two-night summit stay is deeply satisfying.
🥾 5.1 mi descent to dock
🚣 ~1 mi to Cat Island
PrimaryCat Island — 13 mi, 4 sites, dock. The post-summit island camp. Looking back up at Desolation from the lake is something.
AlternateTen Mile Island — 10 mi, 3 sites, no dock. Preferred backup if Cat Island is unavailable; island setting, open lake views.
6
Day
West Shore Exploration — Paddle South Along the West Bank (~7 mi)
The west shoreline of Ross Lake is a different world from the East Bank Trail. Coves, waterfalls dropping off the Skagit Range, and terrain you've never seen from the trail side. Hug the west bank south, stop where the water falls, end the day at Big Beaver for a final evening in the canyon.
🚣 ~7 mi paddle
PrimaryBig Beaver — 4.6 mi, 7 sites, dock. Best camp for a final evening in the Big Beaver Creek canyon.
AlternateRainbow Point — 6 mi from resort, 3 sites, dock. If you want a shorter final morning paddle home.
7
Day
Home — Final Paddle to Resort
Easy morning paddle south to the resort dock. Return kayaks, catch shuttle across, hike up to trailhead. Drive home.
🚣 4–6 mi paddle
🎯 Return to resort
Option C · 7 Days
Desolation + Devil's Dome Side Trip
Two serious summits. Devil's Dome from Devil's Junction on the way north, then one night at Desolation Camp. Island camps bookend the trip.
7 nights ~28 mi paddling 2 major summits Strenuous
1
Day
Marblemount → Short First Paddle (4–5 mi)
Late permit pickup start. Short first day — legs need to be fresh for Devil's Dome on Day 3. Big Beaver Creek exploration by kayak in the evening.
🚣 4–5 mi paddle
PrimaryBig Beaver — 4.6 mi, 7 sites, dock. Best site count south of mid-lake; good first camp.
AlternateMay Creek — 5 mi, 1 site, dock. Quiet solo-style camp just past Big Beaver.
2
Day
Push to Devil's Junction — Dome Prep (4–5 mi)
Shorter paddle day intentionally — arrive early, rest, scout the trailhead, pre-load water for tomorrow's dry climb. Devil's Junction is the launch point for the Dome. Only 1 campsite here, so it's a permit-critical booking.
🚣 4–5 mi paddle
PrimaryDevil's Junction — 9 mi, 1 site only, dock + trail. Dome trailhead. Book this one the moment the window opens.
AlternateRainbow Point — 6 mi, 3 sites, dock. If Junction unavailable — adds 6 miles roundtrip to the Dome hike tomorrow.
3
Day
Devil's Dome Day (~13 mi RT, 5,000 ft)
Earliest possible start. The climb from Devil's Junction to the Dome is relentless and overgrown — first ~3 miles through dense forest with significant blowdowns, then it opens onto Jackita Ridge with staggering views of Jack Mountain and the Picket Range. Dispersed camping at or near the summit is permit-free (Pasayten Wilderness). Option: stay overnight at the Dome for sunrise, then descend Day 4 morning.
🥾 ~13 mi RT
⬆️ ~5,000 ft gain
PrimaryDevil's Junction (return) — Back to the lake camp same day. Long but doable for fit hikers.
Overnight OptionDevil's Dome / Bear Skull area — Dispersed camp near summit, no permit needed in Pasayten. Adds 1 day to itinerary but extraordinary sunrise.
Carry 5L+ water — sources above the Pasayten boundary are unreliable in July. The descent to the lake is described by nearly every trip report as punishing overgrowth. Budget 8+ hours.
4
Day
Recovery Paddle — Devil's Junction → Ten Mile Island (1–2 mi)
Very short paddle north. Legs need a day between the Dome and Desolation. Ten Mile Island is a perfect rest camp — open views, island setting, ~3 miles from the Desolation dock. Rest, swim, eat. Tomorrow is a big day.
🚣 1–2 mi paddle
PrimaryTen Mile Island — 10 mi, 3 sites, no dock. Island rest day. ~3 mi from Desolation dock tomorrow.
AlternatePonderosa — 11 mi, 2 sites, dock. Slightly closer to the Desolation trailhead.
5
Day
Ten Mile → Desolation Camp
Paddle north to the Desolation dock (~3 mi from Ten Mile Island). Stash kayaks and begin the Desolation ascent — different character from the Dome entirely. Drier, more barren, more exposed above treeline. Arrive at Desolation Camp, head to the lookout. Say hello to Jim.
🚣 ~2 mi to dock
🥾 ~4.2 mi to Desolation Camp
SummitDesolation Camp — Separate NPS permit. Carry 4–6L from the lake. No sources on the trail.
6
Day
Descend → Cat Island
Full descent to the Desolation dock, then paddle 1 mile south to Cat Island. Recovery island camp — same move as Option B Day 5. Looking back up at the ridge you climbed twice in a week is its own reward.
🥾 5.1 mi descent
🚣 ~1 mi to Cat Island
PrimaryCat Island — 13 mi, 4 sites, dock. Post-Desolation island recovery. Classic.
AlternateLightning Creek Boat Camp — Stay put if legs are spent. Best facilities on the lake.
7
Day
South to Rainbow Point, then Home
Paddle south past mid-lake coves and waterfalls. Option to push all the way to the resort today (13 mi from Cat Island) or split into a Rainbow Point camp + final morning. Two serious North Cascades summits in one lake trip.
🚣 6–13 mi paddle
🎯 Return to resort
Option: SplitRainbow Point — 6 mi from resort, 3 sites. Camp here, home in the morning (adds Day 8 but gentler exit).
Option: Push throughResort same day — ~13 mi from Cat Island. Long but doable; be off the water by early afternoon.
Heads up on Devil's Dome: The descent from the summit to Devil's Junction is almost universally described as the hardest part — overgrown, significant blowdowns, no mercy. Budget 8+ hours for the full RT. A rest day between the Dome (Day 3) and Desolation (Day 5) is not optional — it's baked into this itinerary as Day 4 on Ten Mile Island.
Logistics
Permit & Reservation Sequence (April 15 Lottery Day)
  • 1
    Lottery spots — NPS recreation.gov backcountry permits On April 15, book in this priority order: (1) Cat Island and Ten Mile Island — island camps fill fast; (2) Lightning Creek Boat Camp — Desolation trailhead camp; (3) Desolation Camp on the mountain — this is a separate permit category from lake camps, book simultaneously; (4) Devil's Junction if doing Option C — only 1 site, will disappear immediately; (5) alternates: Dry Creek, Ponderosa, Rainbow Point, Big Beaver as fallbacks for any unavailable primary. Have your full itinerary pre-planned so you can move fast across multiple nights.
  • 2
    Ross Lake Resort kayak reservation Call (206) 386-4437 or visit rosslakeresort.com. Confirm kayak rental dates and number of boats. Reservations required — especially July. Confirm portage shuttle from the lower dock to resort.
  • 3
    Walk-up permits at Marblemount WIC on departure morning Even with a recreation.gov reservation, you pick up physical permits at the Wilderness Information Center in Marblemount the day of your trip. Opens 8am. Bring printed/digital confirmation. Budget 30–45 min here. Drive time from Seattle: ~2.5 hrs to Marblemount, then another 30 min to the trailhead.
  • 4
    Ross Dam trailhead parking Park at the Ross Lake/Dam Trailhead, milepost 134 on SR-20. Don't leave valuables. Hike 1 mi down to the dock, call the resort from the landline phone ($4/person each way for the shuttle). Shuttle operates 8am–7pm.
